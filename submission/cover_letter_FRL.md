**What the letter does.** LLM-based sentiment scoring is becoming standard infrastructure in empirical finance, and the bias literature around it is growing fast (look-ahead bias, memorization, miscalibration). What has been missing is a *within-design, joint* quantification of the non-semantic channels that a sentiment score rides on: whose name is in the text, which headlines share the API call, and how much of the score is sampling noise. We audit one short-horizon predictability design on 542 search-collected headlines (36 U.S. large-cap firms, July–October 2026), scored by GLM-4-Plus across five sittings and thirteen re-scorings, with each channel varied one ingredient at a time and evaluated with call-clustered inference and a formal equivalence battery.

**Why the findings matter to your readers.**

1. *The identity effect is a placeholder effect, not a company prior.* De-identification raises score levels by two to three points, but swapping company A's name for company B's moves levels by essentially nothing — the decisive control cleanly separates "a name was removed" from "which company it was," resolving a question the look-ahead/memorization literature has debated.
2. *Batch composition is a new, quantified measurement channel.* Scoring the identical headlines inside different API batches moves levels by 3.6 points, replicates with the run order inverted, defies a linear-anchoring mechanism (bounded below 1%), and has sharp downstream costs: regression slopes and ranks survive, but a fixed +50 threshold doubles its trade count and daily sign changes flip on up to a quarter of trading days.
3. *The instrument is stochastic at the serving stack.* ICC 0.92, 30% exact replication across four calls, and temperature-zero decoding restores bitwise determinism for only 83% of headlines — a caution for any pipeline treating a single call as "the" score.
4. *Inference discipline matched to AI-generated covariates.* The letter distinguishes non-rejection from equivalence throughout: four identity manipulations leave predictive content formally equivalent (TOST at a pre-registered margin), the LLM-versus-Loughran–McDonald contrast is honestly reported as bidirectionally undecidable at this sample size, and the echo-free subsample is not claimed equivalent to zero. This operationalizes the inference guidance of Christensen and Hansen (2026, AEA P&P) inside an LLM-finance application.
