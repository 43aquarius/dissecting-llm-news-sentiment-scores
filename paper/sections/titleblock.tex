\noindent\textbf{Abstract.} Do LLM sentiment scores on firm news predict stock returns because of what the text \emph{means}, or because of what the scorer \emph{brings to} the text~---and to the batch? We audit a short-horizon predictability design on 542 search-collected headlines about 36 large U.S.\ firms (341 in-window, July--October 2026), scored by GLM-4-Plus under conditions each varying one ingredient: identity visible, removed, or swapped; repeat calls; controlled batch composition; temperature-zero decoding; and the Loughran--McDonald dictionary. The baseline next-day association is marginal ($t=2.10$, BH $q=0.062$) and draw-dependent across fourteen re-scorings. The robust findings concern measurement: de-identification~---replacing the firm's real name~---raises score levels by 2.6 points pooled across three sessions (95\% CI 1.1 to 4.1; a fictional-name control points to about half, a point-estimate reading), predictive content formally equivalent; swapped names barely move levels; batch composition~---which headlines share an API call~---moves levels by 3.6 points, a channel not, to our knowledge, previously quantified; the instrument is stochastic (ICC $0.92$; 30\% exact replication; temperature zero does not restore determinism); predictability directionally concentrates in the 15\% of price-echo headlines ($p=0.10$); and news coverage selects on realized volatility ($t=2.76$). Anonymized, repeated, batch-frozen, session-interleaved scoring should precede deploying LLM sentiment as a predictor.\par
